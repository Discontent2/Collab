The city had been dead longer than any creature could remember.

Great towers of weathered stone rose above a jungle that had swallowed its streets. Roots split the ancient walls. Trees grew from collapsed rooftops. At the center of the city stood a black fortress, its enormous gates sealed beneath centuries of earth and tangled vines.

Nothing lived inside the fortress.

Nothing that was ever seen.

Yet every creature in the valley knew the ancient ruins belonged to someone.

And on the evening the red tyrant returned, the entire jungle fell silent.

From a ridge overlooking the abandoned city, D-Rex surveyed his kingdom.

His enormous body was scarred from countless battles. Dark markings crossed his crimson hide, and jagged bony ridges crowned his massive skull. Below him lay the hunting grounds where rival predators had gathered in his absence.

He lowered his head.

The scent of unfamiliar animals rose from the courtyard.

Intruders.

D-Rex opened his jaws and released a roar that rolled across the broken towers.

Far below, a young green raptor named Rook froze beside a shattered column.

The smaller dinosaur had never seen the red tyrant before, but the reaction of the other animals told him everything.

An old stegosaur lowered its armored head. Several little carnivores disappeared into the rubble. Even the insects seemed to stop singing.

Only one creature refused to retreat.

A great blue-gray predator stepped into the open courtyard.

It was nearly as tall as D-Rex, with powerful hind legs and a long, muscular skull. Its slate-colored hide bore the marks of battles fought in distant territories.

The newcomer stood over a broken stairway and raised its head.

Then it roared.

The sound was unmistakable.

A challenge.

D-Rex descended into the courtyard.

His great claws struck the ancient paving stones, sending chips of rock skittering into the shadows. The blue predator approached from the opposite side, swinging its tail slowly to maintain balance.

Between them stood the old stegosaur.

For a moment, neither carnivore moved.

D-Rex lowered his skull until his yellow eye was level with his rival's.

The blue dinosaur answered by lifting its head higher.

Both opened their jaws.

Rook watched from the shadow of a collapsed statue.

The little raptor knew what would happen next.

One of the giants would attack. One would fall. The other would claim the ruins.

But before either predator struck, something moved beneath the courtyard.

A low vibration passed through the ground.

It was barely noticeable at first, no louder than distant thunder.

Then the stones trembled.

The stegosaur lifted its head.

D-Rex stopped growling.

The blue predator continued its challenge, apparently unaware that the red tyrant was no longer looking at it.

D-Rex was staring toward the sealed fortress.

Something had changed.

A flock of dark birds exploded from the ancient towers.

A terrible grinding noise echoed through the deserted streets.

And from beneath a collapsed archway, an enormous shape began pulling itself into the evening light.

The first thing Rook saw was its armor.

Huge irregular plates covered its back, packed so tightly that they resembled broken pieces of a stone wall. Beneath them stretched a monstrous reptilian body, darker than the ruins themselves.

Then its head emerged.

The creature possessed the jaws of a giant carnivore, lined with crooked, yellowed teeth. Heavy bony growths surrounded its eyes and neck.

Its scales were coated in the gray dust of the underground chambers.

It had not seen daylight in a very long time.

A pale dinosaur with a brilliant rust-red crest stepped forward to confront the intruder.

The pale predator was smaller than D-Rex, but its head was high and its jaws were already open.

It snapped at the armored creature's face.

The newcomer did not flinch.

The pale dinosaur lunged again.

This time, the armored beast swung its enormous skull sideways.

The impact sent the challenger stumbling through a pile of broken masonry.

Stone shattered.

A roar erupted from the armored creature's throat, a sound unlike anything the living dinosaurs had heard.

Rook flattened himself against the ground.

The blue predator backed away.

Even the old stegosaur retreated, positioning its plated body between the monster and the smaller animals.

But D-Rex stepped forward.

He had recognized the intruder.

Not by sight.

By its scent.

By the peculiar scraping noise of its armored feet.

By the terrible vibration rising through the city.

For the first time since entering the courtyard, the red tyrant showed something that resembled fear.

He remembered that sound.

Long ago, when he had been scarcely larger than Rook, something had pursued his family through these same ruins.

There had been darkness.

Falling stones.

And terrible jaws emerging from beneath the earth.

D-Rex had escaped.

The others had not.

Now the creature had returned.

The armored predator advanced toward the smaller dinosaurs.

Rook stood motionless in its path.

The creature lowered its head.

Its jaws opened.

Then the ground shook beneath a thunderous impact.

D-Rex had charged forward.

His enormous foot struck the paving stones between Rook and the armored creature. He threw back his crimson head and unleashed the loudest roar the ruins had heard in generations.

The armored monster answered.

Across the courtyard, the pale dinosaur struggled upright.

The blue-gray predator approached again, uncertain whether D-Rex was defending the others or attempting to claim the newcomer as its next victim.

The stegosaur raised its great plated back.

Smaller creatures gathered among the fallen columns.

Every dinosaur was watching.

Every dinosaur was waiting.

Then Rook noticed something none of the giants had seen.

Behind the armored creature, the ancient fortress doors were moving.

A narrow black opening had appeared between the great slabs of stone.

Inside that darkness, something scraped against the walls.

Once.

Twice.

Then came a tremendous blow from within, and a large piece of the sealed gateway cracked apart.

D-Rex turned his head toward the sound.

The armored creature stopped advancing.

Deep beneath the ruins, another roar answered.

Then another.

And another.

Rook stared into the darkness as a terrible realization crept over him.

The armored monster had not come to conquer the city.

It had come out of the fortress.

And something had followed it.

The earth began to tremble.

D-Rex opened his jaws.

The ancient kingdom answered with a sound like mountains breaking apart.
