## Premise

The animals do not speak. Narration may provisionally name them, but in-world characterization comes through posture, warning calls, hunting choices, scent recognition, territorial behavior and hesitation before violence.

## D-Rex — The Red Tyrant

- **Silhouette:** Gigantic scarred crimson tyrannosaur, disproportionately powerful head and neck, recognizable ridged crown, heavy tail, forward-driven gait.
- **Distinct features:** Dark red marks, orange-yellow eye, worn and torn skin, deeply inked musculature.
- **Territorial attitude:** Assumes ruins belong to whoever can hold them. Intimidates rivals; not automatically compassionate.
- **Emotional contradiction:** Protects a territory that includes vulnerable smaller animals, while remaining dangerous to them.
- **Known history (draft):** Escaped an armored Gravenmaw attack as a juvenile; his mother and family did not. He recognizes an old predator's scar, scent, movements and resonance.
- **Story arc:** Solitary dominant ruler → apparent antagonist in public challenge → calculating opponent of buried Gravenmaw → reluctant defender of threatened surface habitat.
- **Acting cues:** Low head, narrowed eye, stationary threat displays, long measured steps, deliberate tactical retreat, silence before charge.

## Rook — The Young Survivor

- **Silhouette:** Small lean olive- or dark-green raptor, agile posture; red stripes/spines where consistent with earlier images.
- **Role:** Primary small-scale point of view and audience emotional anchor.
- **Strengths:** Reads other animals, senses danger, observes patterns, can move through spaces unreachable by giants.
- **Limitations:** Physically vulnerable, easily pinned by debris or larger hunters; a mistake is costly.
- **Story arc:** Afraid lone scout → witness to the territorial dispute → recognizes Gravenmaw's sensitivity to ground vibration → ventures into the underworld / helps surviving animals escape in the planned series.
- **Acting cues:** Peering from architectural cover, head tilt, planted claws, retreat then investigative approach; short alarm calls.

## Slate — The Challenger

- **Silhouette:** Blue-gray or slate-blue tyrannosaur-like predator, muscular with a distinct colder palette and skull contour; avoid accidental resemblance to D-Rex.
- **Role:** Competitor for dominance in the ruins.
- **Motivation:** Territorial ambition; his first reading of D-Rex is rival, not protector.
- **Story arc:** Public challenger → reckless fighter who discovers Gravenmaw armor resists ordinary jaws → grudging recognition that he and D-Rex face the same immediate threat.
- **Acting cues:** Raised head and throat, extended neck, answering roars, firm planted feet, pride delaying retreat.

## Ashcrest — The Fearless Hunter

- **Silhouette:** Smaller pale beige predatory dinosaur with a memorable rust-red crest or mane along the skull and neck.
- **Role:** Reckless challenger to armored newcomer; secondary action protagonist.
- **Story arc:** Attacks too soon; learns that blind courage is insufficient against unfamiliar anatomy; later may defend an escape route.
- **Acting cues:** Elevated crest, impulsive lunges, low stalking and abrupt defensive corrections.

## Old Stegosaur — The Shield

- **Silhouette:** Conventional broad-backed herbivore with recognizable large orderly dorsal plates and dangerous tail.
- **Role:** Older animal that often stations itself between smaller dinosaurs and threatening predators.
- **Use:** Provides scale, ecosystem diversity and non-carnivore stakes; should behave as defensive animal, not a military commander.
- **Critical distinction:** Herbivorous stegosaur has a different jaw/head and orderly plates; Gravenmaw is a dark low-bodied four-legged *carnivore* with irregular osteoderms and giant teeth.

## Gravenmaw — Ancient Apex Predator

- **Silhouette:** Dark charcoal, low-slung armored quadrupedal carnivore with reinforced wedge skull, interlocking teeth, irregular bony scutes.
- **Origin:** Natural ancient lineage surviving over generations in caves beneath the city, displaced by flood.
- **Story role:** Material threat and misunderstanding: surface dinosaurs encounter competitors fleeing a flooded homeland.
- **Behavioral signature:** Reads vibration through stone; short crushing rushes, sluggish pivots, vulnerable underside of neck, stopping all noise before strike.
- **Continuity anchor:** One adult has a pale scar over its left eye from D-Rex's juvenile encounter.
- **Full design:** [Gravenmaw Bestiary](creature-gravenmaw-ancient-apex-predator.md).

## Gravenmaw Matriarch — The Buried Crown

- **Silhouette:** Vast female, ancient scarred armor, head broader than fortress gateway in dramatic image, one eye under a massive bone shelf.
- **Motivation:** Survival and defense of brood through the displacement of an ancient underground habitat, not conquest.
- **Cliffhanger function:** Shifts the story from one armored monster to a population and an ecosystem-level upheaval.
- **Reveal rule:** Do not show entire creature in the first chapter. Save scale comparison for a full-size splash composition.
- **Arc resolution:** Redirection toward an unoccupied valley is tentatively preferred over simple extermination.

## Physical Storytelling Rules

1. No human dialogue or cartoon grimaces for dinosaurs.
2. Distinguish territorial warning roars from pain, alarm and charging.
3. Treat eye line and angle of skull as meaning; angle changes precede movement.
4. Maintain physical weight: feet plant, hips shift and tails counterbalance.
5. Keep each creature color, armor and proportions consistent across panels and animation.
6. Use partial occlusion and architectural cover for believable cinematic transitions.
7. Gravenmaw are powerful animals with understandable ecological motivation, not servants or soldiers.
