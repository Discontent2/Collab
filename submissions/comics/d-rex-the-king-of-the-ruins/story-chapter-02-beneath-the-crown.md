The first stone fell without a sound.

It broke free from the ancient fortress wall and struck the courtyard, disappearing between the enormous feet of the gathered dinosaurs.

Then came another.

And another.

Rook watched a thin crack spread across the sealed gateway.

The great armored predator stood motionless before it, its immense head turned toward the darkness. Its bony plates shifted with each breath. Its jaws were open, but the terrible growling had stopped.

For several moments, no creature moved.

The red tyrant stood at the center of the courtyard, his crimson hide glowing in the last light of evening. Across from him, Slate crouched beside the collapsed remains of a stone staircase.

Both predators stared at the fortress.

Deep beneath their feet, something answered the armored beast's earlier call.

A long, trembling roar rose from the earth.

It seemed to come from everywhere.

The old stegosaur backed toward the smaller dinosaurs, swinging its heavy tail defensively.

Ashcrest raised its pale head.

Rook pressed himself against a fallen column.

Then the fortress gate exploded outward.

A tremendous slab of masonry crashed into the courtyard. Dust and shattered stone filled the air. Cold water poured through the opening, spreading across the ancient pavement in a dark, muddy sheet.

From within the cloud of dust emerged the silhouette of another armored creature.

It was smaller than the first, but possessed the same enormous skull, the same low shoulders, the same jagged armor.

Its eyes were almost closed against the unfamiliar evening light.

The first Gravenmaw turned toward it.

The two animals touched their heavy snouts.

For an instant, their behavior was unexpectedly gentle.

A low vibration passed between them.

Then the second creature turned toward the gathered dinosaurs, opened its jaws, and roared.

Ashcrest attacked.

The pale predator launched itself across the shattered courtyard, its rust-colored crest raised high. Its claws struck the newcomer's shoulder armor, producing a terrible scraping sound.

The armored beast twisted.

Ashcrest lost its footing.

With a sideways sweep of its massive skull, Gravenmaw struck the smaller predator beneath the ribs and hurled it against a broken wall.

The impact sent a shower of stone fragments across the courtyard.

Ashcrest collapsed, stunned.

Slate charged.

The blue-gray tyrannosaur struck the first Gravenmaw from the side, sinking its teeth into the creature's shoulder.

The result was not what Slate expected.

The teeth slid across the overlapping bone plates. One broke against the hard surface.

The armored predator turned with surprising force and slammed its reinforced skull into Slate's chest.

The blue-gray dinosaur staggered backward, its claws gouging the pavement.

D-Rex watched.

He did not rush to help.

Instead, he circled the struggling creatures, keeping his massive head low and his yellow eyes fixed on the Gravenmaw's movements.

The ancient predator turned slowly, following Slate.

Its heavy armor made its shoulders nearly impenetrable.

But every time it raised its head, a narrow fold of unprotected flesh appeared beneath its throat.

D-Rex stopped moving.

The Gravenmaw sensed the vibration of his last footstep and turned.

For one terrible moment, the two giants faced each other across the courtyard.

D-Rex recognized the scar above the armored predator's left eye.

A long, pale groove interrupted the black osteoderms.

He had seen it before.

Years earlier, as a juvenile, D-Rex had followed his mother into a narrow valley beyond the eastern ruins.

The ground there had been wet and dark. A stream flowed from beneath a stone overhang.

Something enormous had emerged from the water.

His mother had fought it.

He remembered the sound of her jaws closing over bone.

He remembered the armored skull rising through the falling rain.

And he remembered running.

Now that same skull faced him again.

D-Rex roared.

The Gravenmaw lowered its head and charged.

The collision shook the entire courtyard.

D-Rex caught the ancient predator across the side of its armored neck, but his teeth failed to penetrate the thick bone plates.

The Gravenmaw thrust forward, driving the red tyrant backward through a collapsed arch.

Stone blocks toppled around them.

D-Rex twisted free.

The Gravenmaw pursued.

Its short, powerful limbs hammered the ground, its armored spine cutting through the dust.

For the first time, the red tyrant retreated.

Not in panic.

He was drawing the heavier creature away from the open courtyard.

Away from the smaller animals.

Toward the narrow passage between two ruined towers.

Rook watched from a shattered staircase.

The little raptor had noticed something strange.

Every time the Gravenmaw struck the ground, the second armored creature hesitated, lifting its skull and turning toward the resulting vibrations.

Both predators reacted more quickly to sound traveling through stone than to the movements of nearby animals.

Rook crouched.

Far below the staircase, water seeped from a crack in the paving.

He placed one claw against the stone.

A rhythmic trembling traveled upward through his foot.

Something large was moving beneath him.

The little dinosaur gave a short alarm call.

No one heard it.

In the narrow passage, D-Rex suddenly pivoted.

His heavy tail struck a broken pillar.

The Gravenmaw lunged past him, unable to turn quickly enough.

D-Rex seized the exposed skin beneath its neck.

For the first time, his teeth found flesh.

The armored predator screamed.

It thrashed violently, smashing its shoulder against the crumbling tower. Stone fragments showered down over both creatures.

D-Rex tightened his grip.

The Gravenmaw's claws gouged deep trenches into the ancient pavement.

Then a tremendous crack sounded overhead.

The tower began to fall.

D-Rex released his grip and stumbled backward.

The Gravenmaw twisted, trying to escape the narrow passage.

Both giants disappeared beneath an avalanche of collapsing masonry.

For several heartbeats, nothing moved.

Then the dust began to settle.

A crimson tail emerged from the rubble.

D-Rex dragged himself free, his hide torn and bleeding.

Across the passage, the armored creature lay partly buried beneath the shattered tower.

Its enormous sides rose and fell.

It was alive.

Rook stood at the top of the broken staircase and watched the red tyrant approach his trapped enemy.

D-Rex lowered his head.

The Gravenmaw attempted to rise.

Its jaws opened.

D-Rex bared his teeth.

He could finish the fight.

But from beneath the city came another call.

It was deeper than the others.

The wounded Gravenmaw stopped struggling.

Its head turned toward the distant sound.

D-Rex looked toward the fortress.

The courtyard was trembling again.

At first, the movement appeared to be another earthquake. Then Rook noticed that the water spreading across the stone was flowing backward.

It was being pulled toward the open gateway.

Something had changed in the underground passages.

The younger Gravenmaw began retreating into the fortress, its great claws slipping on the wet pavement.

Slate emerged from the rubble.

Ashcrest struggled to its feet.

Even the stegosaur turned toward the opening.

A huge section of the courtyard suddenly collapsed.

The ground dropped away, revealing a shaft that descended into unimaginable darkness.

Cold air rushed upward.

Then came the smell.

Wet stone.

Ancient mud.

Blood.

And something else.

The overwhelming scent of a vast gathering of living animals.

Deep below the courtyard, enormous shapes were moving through an underground river.

Their armored backs broke the black water.

One after another.

Rook could see at least six.

Then a seventh shape moved beneath them.

It was much larger.

The smaller Gravenmaw were not attacking the city as a conquering army. They were escaping their flooded home.

And whatever lived in the deepest chamber was driving them ahead of it.

A massive head rose slowly through the darkness.

Its skull was broader than the gateway itself.

Its bony armor was broken and worn, covered with old scars. One enormous eye opened beneath a shelf of ancient bone.

The creature inhaled.

Then it released a roar so deep that the water trembled in the collapsed shaft.

D-Rex stood motionless.

Slate lowered his head.

Ashcrest retreated into the ruins.

Even the wounded Gravenmaw struggled desperately to free itself from the rubble.

Rook understood only one thing.

The animal climbing toward the surface was larger than anything that had ever walked through the courtyard.

And it was coming.

For the first time, the red tyrant and his rivals stood facing the same enemy.

Above them, the last sunlight vanished behind the towers.

The buried kingdom was opening.

And the oldest hunger in the world was rising to meet the night.
