## Central Premise

The strongest creature in the kingdom may not be its rightful king. Ancient ruins shelter surface dinosaurs competing for territory. Beneath them lies an older, self-sustaining ecological world. A change in an underground river floods the caverns and drives armored apex predators upward. D-Rex has survived one of these beasts before and is forced to confront a threat that does not care about his surface rule.

**Logline:** When an armored lineage older than the surface kingdom emerges from the flooded underworld, a brutal red tyrannosaur must face his oldest fear while rivals and smaller dinosaurs struggle to survive the collapse of a forgotten city.

**Central question:** Is D-Rex fighting to conquer the ruins, or prevent something inside them from escaping?

### Issue #2 — The River Below

- Gravenmaw flood into the city's ruined districts.
- D-Rex and Slate become reluctant allies against an immediate habitat-level threat.
- Rook descends through a fractured stair or river passage and discovers the flooded nesting caverns.
- The ancient fortress's function as an ecological barrier becomes legible through environmental evidence.
- Cross-cut underwater/subterranean exploration against desperate surface defense.
- **Cliffhanger:** The matriarch fully reaches the surface and threatens the small dinosaurs' remaining sanctuary.

### Issue #3 — The Buried Crown

- D-Rex confronts the matriarch as fortress towers and courtyards collapse.
- Slate, Ashcrest and the plated stegosaur defend the surface escape path.
- Rook uses his knowledge of fissures, the watercourse and Gravenmaw behavior to help redirect the displaced creatures.
- Rather than 'slaying an evil queen,' the climax recognizes that the old predators are escaping a damaged habitat.
- Many animals reach an unoccupied valley through a river corridor; the surface refuge survives in altered form.
- **Final image:** D-Rex stands over the ruined city as enormous armored bodies move downstream into unexplored wilderness, implying the struggle for territory will continue.

## Worldbuilding: The Two Kingdoms

### Surface kingdom
- Jungle, sunset, abandoned masonry, monumental towers and courtyards.
- Ruled informally by present-day predator displays; D-Rex acts as territorial king.
- Heavily structured architecture contrasted with living reptilian shapes.
- Minor dinosaurs depend on architectural cover and larger animals' movements for safety.

### Subterranean kingdom
- Deep black lakes, old watercourses, natural caverns, fungal patches, ancient mineral formations and isolated food web.
- Gravenmaw evolved/persevered there **over successive generations**.
- Floods reroute predator and prey populations into human-built or ancient constructed passages.
- The civilization closed the access corridor after repeated encounters; over time the surface animals forgot.

**Scientific framing:** Prehistoric survival is fictional but its ecological logic should feel coherent. Do not imply immortal individuals or literal scientific proof of this imaginary species.

## Visual Production Lock

See the [existing Art Style Guide](art-style-guide.md). Do not revise its established 1975–1980 Bronze Age comic aesthetic.

### Surface color keys
- Crimson `#A92E21`, rust `#D76A40`, amber `#EBA75C`, parchment `#EACB81`, slate `#566D7C`, ink `#202A31`.
- Dramatic low angles, halftone, yellow narration boxes, heavy crosshatching, physical stop-motion creature aesthetic.

### Underworld color keys
- Ink-dark `#111B20`, deep slate `#26383B`, stone `#445458`, mineral gray `#73817C`, bone light `#B2AD92`.
- Reflective underground water and negative space, narrow beams and fragmentary glimpses of scale.

### Gravenmaw staging
- Gradual anatomical reveal before a full shot: rough plates, feet, jaw, eye, head, whole body.
- The matriarch's introduction lasts longer: moving underwater ridge → eye beneath bony shelf → massive head behind fortress → monumental reveal.
- Panel priority: coherent physical weight and recognizable location, not arbitrary explosions and roaring.

## Audio and Motion Heritage

The source concept developed from 8-second transitions of the stop-motion-inspired D-REX short. The comic should preserve:
- Silent creature acting; body language drives emotional meaning.
- Deliberate articulated movements and miniature-set textures evocative of Harryhausen-era practical effects.
- Roar as distinct communication: territorial challenge, warning, defensive alarm, pain.
- Pacing through uneasy stillness before violent motion.
