## One-Paragraph Summary

The **Waterfall Spine-Tyrant** is a huge, muscular, saurian-adjacent riparian creature found near plunge pools, drowned ravines, waterfall caves, and heavy jungle runoff zones. Its body is built for impact: thick armored hide, oversized clawed hands, a powerful tail, ridge-spines along the neck and back, and a chest like wet stone slabs. Witnesses describe it as a tyrant-lizard that learned to live inside falling water. It is not a confirmed apex monster or engineered war beast. In intake-safe terms, it is a territorial waterfall guardian, route hazard, and pressure creature whose violence is usually tied to intrusion, loud machinery, blocked water, or people trying to claim its pool.

## Visual Description

A towering reptilian brawler with pale gray-blue scaled skin, black-blue striping, heavy musculature, long claws, and a raised line of dark dorsal spines from skull crest to tail. Water slicks across its armor-like hide, collecting in cracks between scale plates and running off its shoulders in sheets. Its head is long-snouted and predatory, but its posture is almost wrestler-like: low center of gravity, wide arms, braced legs, and a tail coiled for balance in wet stone terrain.

In silhouette, it reads as a living cliff-face with teeth. When it steps from the waterfall spray, the spines appear first, then the eyes, then the hands.

## Habitat

Likely encounter zones include:

- jungle waterfalls
- plunge pools
- wet ravines
- cave mouths behind falling water
- flooded route cuts
- hidden springs below cliffs
- old hydro-control ruins
- heavy runoff corridors

It prefers water-rich thresholds where sound, mist, stone, and territorial scent are constantly being washed and renewed.

## Behavior

The Waterfall Spine-Tyrant is territorial, but not mindlessly hostile. It uses the roar of falling water as cover, standing inside spray curtains until intruders come too close. It may strike the pool surface to produce warning booms, drag logs or stones into rough barricades, and shove carcasses or refuse away from the main current.

It feeds on large fish, carrion, river reptiles, fungal mats near warm stone, mineral-rich bone, and animals washed into plunge pools during storms. It may also chew waterlogged machine parts for mineral residue, which causes World Works crews to misclassify it as infrastructure-hostile.

## Relationship To People

Local route workers give waterfall pools a wide berth if they find:

- fresh claw gouges in slick stone
- spines scraping wet cave ceilings
- big handprints in mud near plunge pools
- shattered intake pipes
- logs arranged across water access
- sudden silence from smaller animals near the falls

World Works reports tend to call it a hazard species, obstruction animal, or riparian asset-threat. Some black-market hunters sell fake dorsal spines as strength charms. Antisapian stories may treat its waterfall roar as a warning, not a challenge.

Aeonolacertian route-keepers might read it as a threshold creature: the water has a body here, and that body is not cargo access.

## Encounter Use

Possible encounter patterns:

- A crew follows a hidden water source and finds the creature standing motionless inside the falls.
- A route repair team tries to reopen an intake pipe the Spine-Tyrant has blocked with logs and stone.
- A traveler needs clean water, but the pool belongs to something that will not be rushed.
- A fight begins as a misunderstanding when engine noise sounds like a rival challenge.
- A non-combat scene involves lowering tools, backing away from the pool, and waiting for the creature to stop displaying.

## Weakness / Non-Combat Handling

Best handled through distance and water etiquette.

Safe approaches:

- shut off engines near plunge pools
- avoid direct approach through the main spray line
- do not step between the creature and deep water
- do not block runoff channels
- avoid shouting over the falls
- lower tools, poles, and weapons
- retreat slowly along dry stone instead of splashing backward
- leave water access without taking more than needed

Its size makes combat dangerous. Its real weakness is territorial predictability: if given space, it often chooses display over pursuit.
